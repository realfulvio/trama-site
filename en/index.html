Where Trama works differently

We do not claim “more methods than anyone”: coverage is still partial, and we say so below. These are the points where Trama works differently from traditional commercial software, and where to check each one.

  • Free, no licences to manage

    No cost, subscription or seat count. MIT licence: use it, study it and modify it, including in professional work.

    Check: LICENSE

  • It just starts

    Extract the ZIP folder and run Trama.exe. No Python to install, no account to create, no activation key to enter.

    Check: getting started (it)

  • Your data stays on your computer

    The application source imports no network module: no telemetry, no sync, no online licence check.

    Check: search urllib, socket

  • A readable project file

    In the simple case a .alab project is JSON text: open it in any editor, compare it with a diff, archive it without depending on the program.

    Check: sample file

  • Method and limits written into the result

    An exact test says when it switched to an approximation and why. An interval that cannot be computed is explained, not filled in. The robust fit is descriptive: no invented standard errors or p-values. Analysis from summaries does not reconstruct artificial observations.

    Check: features (it)

  • Data, analyses and graphs stay linked

    Edit a value and the linked analyses recalculate. An excluded cell stays in the sheet and is left out of the calculation, not deleted.

    Check: first experiment (it)

  • Vector figures for papers and posters

    Export to SVG, PDF (also multipage), PNG or TIFF. In native layouts, up to 12 panels, compatible vector parts stay vector; raster parts stay images.

    Check: graphs and figures (it)

  • Public verification, limits included

    Verification reports record the test environment, test outcomes and portable ZIP checksum. The link below is the 0.94 report.

    0.94 verification

Trama and traditional commercial software

The comparison is about how you work, not about a list of methods. The right-hand column describes what often happens: it does not refer to any particular product.

What you can do

Six families of tools in 0.107.0 beta, from the same worksheet. Open the full map of formats and features.

Compare experiments

t-tests (also lognormal and TOST equivalence), exact rank tests, ordinary, Welch and Brown–Forsythe ANOVA, repeated measures, REML mixed models with Kenward–Roger, multiple comparisons and compact letters on the graph.

Fit and compare curves

Asymmetric parameter profiles; 118 catalogue equations in 14 families, custom and conditional equations with fixed thresholds, global fits with shared parameters, weighting, AICc comparison, direct and inverse calibration, splines, LOWESS and robust fits.

Explore complex data

PCA with biplot, Lloyd and Hartigan–Wong K-means with a 17-index consensus and reports for each k, hierarchical clustering with dendrograms, merge maps and WCSS elbow plots, correlation matrices, heat maps and confidence ellipses.

Study outcomes and associations

Kaplan–Meier, Cox and competing risks, ROC curves with DeLong comparison, Bland–Altman, multiple, logistic and Poisson regression, contingency tables and diagnostic measures.

Prepare observations

In-sheet formulas, transformations, baselines also with replicates, normalisation, area under the curve and peaks, Monte Carlo studies with a reproducible seed.

Compose the figure

XY and column graphs, box and violin plots, forest and bubble plots, asymmetric error bars, native annotations and shapes, layouts up to 12 panels.

What Trama is not (yet)

Better to know before you choose.

  • It is a beta. 0.x versions can contain errors and incomplete features; there is no stable release yet.
  • Partial coverage. We do not claim equivalence with other programs: an established package may offer options, formats, official support and years of maturity that Trama does not have.
  • Windows 10/11 64-bit only for the portable package.
  • Importing projects from other programs is limited to verified content; there is no universal export. See compatibility (Italian).
  • The interface is available in Italian and English, but some result tables, legends and messages remain in English.
  • No clinical validation or regulatory certification. Tests prove the cases that were run, not every possible use.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions we hear most often.

Is Trama really free?
Yes. Trama is released under the MIT license: no cost, subscription or per-seat limit, including for professional use.
Which systems does it run on and how do I install it?
The portable package is for 64-bit Windows 10 and 11. Extract the ZIP folder and run Trama.exe: no Python, account or activation key is needed. You need a screen of at least 1024 × 768 pixels.
Which statistical analyses and graphs does it offer?
t tests (including lognormal and equivalence TOST), exact rank tests, ANOVA, repeated measures and mixed models, linear, logistic and nonlinear regression with 118 catalogued equations, global fits, Kaplan–Meier, Cox and competing risks, ROC curves, PCA, K-means and hierarchical clustering. Graphs include XY, columns, box and violin plots, heat maps, forest and bubble plots, exported as SVG, PDF, PNG and TIFF.
Can I import my data from Excel or CSV?
Yes: you can paste from Excel and import CSV, TSV, TXT and XLSX files (sheets are imported as data). The native .alab project is JSON text that opens in any editor.
Does Trama collect data or need an internet connection?
No. The application source has no network modules: no telemetry, no sync and no online license check. Your data stays on your computer.
Can I use it for clinical or regulatory results?
Trama is a beta with partial coverage and no clinical validation or regulatory certification. Always verify the results; the limits are written on the page and in the release notes.
Where is the source code?
On GitHub, together with the quick guide, the feature list and the release history.

Try it in three steps

About five minutes, nothing to install. Needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and a screen of at least 1024 × 768 pixels.

  1. Download

    Get Trama-<version>-Windows-x64.zip from the releases page, not the source archive.

  2. Extract and run

    Extract the whole folder, keep the _internal folder next to the executable and run Trama.exe.

  3. Open the sample

    Download Laboratorio.alab and open it: synthetic data, a global fit and a ready-made layout.
